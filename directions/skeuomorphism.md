# skeuomorphism

real materials and objects (leather, paper, knobs, glass) rendered so controls look like what they do. fits instruments, creative tools, anything tactile.

## sources
- [A documented screenshot collection of early iPhone OS interfaces](https://www.versionmuseum.com/history-of/ios): Compare Notes, Calculator, navigation bars, and controls. Use material cues to communicate function, and keep highlights, bevels, and shadows consistent with one lighting model.
- [Arturia’s Mini V, a software recreation of a physical synthesizer](https://www.arturia.com/products/software-instruments/mini-v/overview): Preserve recognizable control groupings, knob scales, switch positions, and panel labeling. Physical resemblance should help users predict interaction and understand the signal path.
- [Original HP instrument photographs and service-document scans](https://hpmemoryproject.org/wa_pages/wall_a_page_02.htm): Study the hierarchy between meter, range selector, connectors, and labels before drawing a virtual instrument. Derive spacing and surface treatment from a particular object rather than an imagined “vintage machine.”

## avoid
Leather stitching, wood grain, and glossy knobs applied to unrelated tasks without a coherent physical metaphor.
