# museum archive

specimen labels, catalog numbers, plates, captions, archival paper. fits collections, research, libraries, taste pages.

## sources
- [Digitized *Kunstformen der Natur*, with navigable historical illustration plates](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/page/47388491): Arrange related specimens as deliberate comparative groups, with small numbered identifiers and separate explanatory text. Let isolated silhouettes and consistent spacing do the organizing.
- [Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History’s botanical specimen catalog and record interface](https://collections.nmnh.si.edu/search/botany/?irn=2787599): Make accession identifiers, taxonomy, collecting location, and dates explicit fields. Keep the specimen image distinct from its evidentiary metadata; favor repeatable record structures over editorial cards.
- [A complete museum object record with image, material, dimensions, credit line, provenance sections, and accession number](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45434): Separate the human-readable object title from structured catalog facts. Use progressive detail for provenance and references while preserving a stable identifier and a dominant object image.

## avoid
Fake coffee stains, arbitrary accession numbers, typewriter text everywhere, and scrapbook clutter masquerading as cataloging.
