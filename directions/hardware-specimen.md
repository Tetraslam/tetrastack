# hardware specimen

the look of physical machines, with textured controls, cell grids, and technical clarity without hacker costume. fits infrastructure and serious devtools.

## sources
- [Oxide’s product site, combining rack photography, figure labels, console UI, and technical diagrams](https://oxide.computer/): Present hardware as a documented object: isolated views, close crops of fabrication details, small figure captions, and restrained status colors. Let actual tables and configuration screens supply visual density.
- [The manufacturer’s OP–1 field product presentation](https://teenage.engineering/products/op-1): Study the relationship between pale enclosure, tightly grouped controls, and selective color coding. Pair large object photography with compact, explicit feature annotations rather than ornamental dashboard graphics.
- [Photographed early HP voltmeters, original catalog descriptions, and a scanned 1944 service-manual schematic](https://hpmemoryproject.org/wa_pages/wall_a_page_02.htm): Borrow engraved-label hierarchy, graduated scales, model identifiers, and functional group boundaries. The historical reference is the instruments and documents, not the archive website’s styling.

## avoid
Random serial numbers, meaningless crosshairs, and hazard-orange decoration pretending to be engineering information.
