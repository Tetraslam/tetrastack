# memphis

squiggles, terrazzo, clashing pastels and primaries, playful geometry. fits consumer fun, kids, creative play.

## sources
- [Official manufacturer’s page for Ettore Sottsass’s *Carlton* bookcase, 1981](https://www.italianradical.design/en/products/carlton): Combine a stable overall silhouette with contrasting internal directions. Assign flat colors to discrete structural pieces; confine dense pattern to selected surfaces.
- [Official manufacturer’s page for Peter Shire’s *Bel Air* chair, 1982](https://www.italianradical.design/en/products/bel-air): Balance a large asymmetric angular shape against a smaller curved counterweight. Mix pastel and saturated fields at unequal scales instead of distributing every color evenly.
- [Official manufacturer’s page for Sottsass’s *Tahiti* lamp, 1981](https://www.italianradical.design/en/products/tahiti): Contrast a small black-and-white patterned base with broad, untextured yellow, pink, and red parts. Give each shape a recognizable role; playful silhouettes should still have internal organization.

## on the web
- [Memphis Milano, the official furniture and objects site](https://memphis.it/en/): saturated panels, heavy black gutters, and contrasting pattern bands turn the objects' language into page structure. disciplined product framing keeps the exuberance usable.
- [Config, Figma's conference site](https://config.figma.com/): mismatched geometric letterforms and repeated irregular marks give character while event information stays rigorously arranged. memphis-adjacent and mostly monochrome, not pastel squiggles.

## avoid
Confetti squiggles on every surface, or generic “Corporate Memphis” people mistaken for the original movement.
