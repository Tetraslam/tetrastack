# tufte data-ink

maximum data, minimum chrome, small multiples, sparklines, labels instead of legends. fits analytics, research, anything numbers-first.

## sources
- [Tufte’s official book page, with original sample spreads from the foundational statistical-graphics book](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/the-visual-display-of-quantitative-information/): Let data marks carry the visual weight; subordinate rules and axes. Use comparable scales and small multiples instead of decorative chart containers.
- [Tufte’s official *Beautiful Evidence* page with sample spreads](https://www.edwardtufte.com/book/beautiful-evidence/): Place images, quantitative evidence, and explanation together. Use precise annotations near their referents rather than separating “insights” into distant cards.
- [Working web specimen translating Tufte’s book typography and marginalia into responsive HTML](https://edwardtufte.github.io/tufte-css/): Study the main-column/sidenote proportions, ET Book typography, near-white background, and figures that selectively extend beyond the text column. Collapse marginalia deliberately on narrow screens.

## on the web
- [Gwern's site, a long-form reading system](https://gwern.net/): scholarly typography, sidenotes, recursive link previews. the main argument stays readable while supporting material lives in place.
- [Red Blob Games, interactive technical explanations](https://www.redblobgames.com/): diagrams and prose form one teaching interface. controls sit beside the thing they change, and visual encodings stay consistent between diagrams.

## avoid
A beige serif blog labeled “Tufte,” with oversized whitespace and the actual evidence hidden behind tabs.
