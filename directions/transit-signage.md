# transit signage

strict color coding, bold sans labels, lines and stops, arrows. fits navigation, systems maps, routing and status.

## sources
- [Publisher’s reproduction of Vignelli and Noorda’s 1970 NYCTA manual, with photographs and scanned spreads](https://standardsmanual.com/products/nyctacompactedition): Treat arrows, route symbols, text baselines, and padding as one measured system. Repeat consistent horizontal sign bands and reserve route colors for identification.
- [MoMA’s collection record and image of the original NYCTA Graphics Standards Manual, dated 1966–70](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/89303): Study the manual’s own information hierarchy: strong headings, tightly organized specifications, and diagrams that explain relationships without decoration.
- [Transport for London’s official directory of graphics, signage, interchange, and related design standards](https://tfl.gov.uk/info-for/business-and-advertisers/design-standards): Follow the linked standards for symbol clearances, lettering hierarchy, and consistent directional information. Separate destination, direction, and service identity rather than mixing them into one label.

## on the web
none at the bar yet. the Port Authority's wayfinding manual site is a plain document site; the system lives in its PDFs.

## avoid
Random Helvetica, decorative subway lines, and colored circles that do not identify stable destinations or categories.
