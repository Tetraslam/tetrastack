# swiss / international typographic

a rigorous grid, sans-serif type, asymmetric layout, flat color, lots of white space. fits information-heavy products that need clarity.

## sources
- [Josef Müller-Brockmann’s poster for a concert at Tonhalle Zürich, 1953](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/6257): Separate expressive geometric composition from a tightly organized information block. Align text to shared axes and establish hierarchy through size and spacing rather than ornamental containers.
- [Müller-Brockmann’s *Werner Bischof* exhibition poster, 1957](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/8808): Treat photography as a structural part of the grid. Use a decisive crop and an economical sans-serif hierarchy; captions and headings should share alignment even when their scales differ.
- [Mike Joyce’s official archive and shop of Swiss-inspired concert-poster reinterpretations](https://www.swissted.com/): Study the posters’ repeated modules, typographic rhythm, and sharply limited palettes. Translate their systems into repeatable page sections rather than copying a single poster as a full-screen hero.

## on the web
- [Grilli Type, a type foundry with precise margins, restrained navigation, a clear label/content grid, and expressive specimen art direction](https://www.grillitype.com/): keep the interface disciplined so the typography can be adventurous. small labels and generous specimens establish hierarchy without decorative UI.
- [Dinamo, a foundry site with oversized navigation, deliberate empty space, strong specimen presentation, and direct product discovery](https://abcdinamo.com/): combine editorial confidence with useful interactive specimens. borrow its typographic hierarchy rather than treating every flourish as Swiss doctrine.

## avoid
Oversized Helvetica and one red square on a layout with no consistent grid.
