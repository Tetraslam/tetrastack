# native material

the product renders its own pitch in its own medium (ascii in a terminal, a live trace in a trace viewer). fits any product with a strong medium.

## sources
- [Ghostty’s landing page, whose hero is a native-looking terminal window displaying character-based artwork](https://ghostty.org/): Let the product’s own output supply the hero image. Preserve terminal cell proportions, restrained ANSI-like color, and believable window chrome; keep explanatory text and download actions outside the demonstration.
- [Typst’s site, showing document markup alongside typeset output](https://www.typst.app/): Show the transformation the product performs. Align source and result clearly, and let equations, page composition, and typography demonstrate quality instead of generic illustrations.
- [The LaTeX team’s documentation collection, manuals produced in the typesetting system itself](https://www.latex-project.org/help/documentation/): Use a real specimen as proof: running heads, numbered sections, mathematical notation, and cross-references all demonstrate the tool’s competence.

## on the web
- [Ghostty, the terminal emulator's site](https://ghostty.org/): an ascii-art ghost rendered in the terminal is the hero, so the tool's own medium sells it.
- [tldraw, the live whiteboard that is also the SDK's demo](https://www.tldraw.com/): the canvas is the introduction, with carefully placed floating tools and immediate use. lead with the product when a few seconds of making something says more than a landing page.

## avoid
A decorative terminal screenshot unrelated to the product, or claiming a static mockup is a live demonstration.
