# solarpunk

lush green, warm sun, art nouveau curves, optimistic ecology. fits climate, community, and hopeful futures.

## sources
- [Design director Gabrielle Lamontagne’s *Dear Alice* campaign archive, with original artwork and direction](https://gabriellelamontagne.com/Dear-Alice-1): Combine warm agricultural colors with useful, approachable technology. Give infrastructure visible functions and integrate it into inhabited landscapes rather than placing plants around anonymous futuristic towers.
- [A functioning solar-powered publication whose website exposes its energy constraints](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/): Borrow the visible battery state, economical page structure, and restrained image treatment. Make ecological operation legible in the interface instead of expressing sustainability solely through green color.
- [The movement’s own publishing platform, displaying commissioned cover artwork and named contributors](https://solarpunkmagazine.com/): Study illustration-led identity, community-oriented navigation, and editorial variety. Let people, maintenance, and shared life appear alongside vegetation and energy systems.
