# ukiyo-e / woodblock

flat color planes, bold outlines, patterned water and sky, a carved line. fits storytelling, culture, and slow, beautiful products.

## sources
- [The Met’s Hokusai *Under the Wave off Kanagawa*, ca. 1830–32](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45434): Use a few strongly separated color masses, dark contour lines, and large asymmetrical shapes. Borrow the dramatic scale contrast and framing of negative space rather than reproducing the wave.
- [Hiroshige’s *Sudden Shower over Shin-Ōhashi Bridge and Atake*, 1857](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/55433): Study the steep diagonal bridge against broad horizontal fields, localized tonal gradation, and fine rain lines. Keep texture directional and subordinate to the main silhouette.
- [Hiroshige’s *Sudden Shower in Shōno*, ca. 1833–34](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/36967): Layer dark foreground shapes against pale distant forms; use diagonals to direct reading. Keep title cartouches discrete and rectangular instead of scattering ornamental lettering.
- [Hiroshige’s *Mishima: Morning Mist*, ca. 1833–34](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/10926): fog drawn by removing the key-block line. Far forms become flat grey silhouettes, each paler than the one before, with no outline, while near figures keep full line and color.
- [Hiroshige’s *Autumn Moon over Tama River*, ca. 1837–38](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/47805): the moon as an unoutlined pale disc in a blue bokashi, low mist bands over the far shore, water as flat pale ribbons.
- [Hiroshige’s *Kanbara: Evening Snow*, ca. 1833–34](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/25613): night without black. A sumi bokashi from the top, everything else held in two or three greys.
- [Hiroshige’s *Ryōgoku Bridge and the Great Riverbank*, 1856](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/34277): beni bokashi on the horizon at dusk, a bridge in perspective with piers as rhythmic dark verticals.
- [Kawase Hasui’s *Misty Moonlight at Matsue*, 1924](https://www.artic.edu/artworks/2587) (shin-hanga, in copyright): night light done with one lit window, lit cloud edges, broken reflections. Study it for changing light; the edo prints rarely show the same place at different hours.

## learned in practice
- kiri (live golden gate fog): the same view printed as weather states is the genre's native form (tōkaidō morning mist, sudden shower, evening snow). Treat each color as a block: flat fill, slight misregistration, pigment pooling at block edges, woodgrain in large fills, washi fiber over all. Put the key line only on near forms.
